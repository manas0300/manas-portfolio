DHOLAVIRA

Computational Media · Mahindra University

← Back to Projects
Context & Intention

A computational reconstruction translating the archaeological footprint of an ancient Indus Valley metropolis into a realtime, navigable 3D environment.

Dholavira, situated on the island of Khadir Bet in the Great Rann of Kutch, represents one of the most sophisticated urban settlements of the Harappan civilization. Unlike contemporary sites built primarily from fired brick, Dholavira was constructed from massive dressed stone and mud-brick, fortified with monumental perimeter walls and an extraordinary network of rainwater reservoirs.

This project reconstructs the fortified settlement in 3D using Unreal Engine and Blender. The intention behind the reconstruction is experiential: moving beyond flat archaeological schematics and static diagrams to explore how spatial visualization, material authenticity, and atmospheric lighting can communicate the scale, volume, and civil ingenuity of ancient urban planning.

By grounding archaeological research in real-time 3D environments, the project demonstrates how computational media bridges the gap between historical inquiry and immersive digital storytelling—allowing contemporary viewers to inhabit and understand an ancient urban system.

01 / Spatial Reconstruction

Tripartite City Planning & Fortified Architecture

The reconstruction visualizes Dholavira's distinctive tripartite urban plan: a formidable Citadel (Castle and Bailey) occupying the highest vantage, an adjoining Middle Town with planned orthogonal streets and residential blocks, and an expansive Lower Town bounded within peripheral stone walls.

Reconstructed settlement overview: spatial hierarchy, outer perimeter fortifications, and surrounding reservoir basins.

The environment was constructed using modular stone masonry assets designed to capture the rhythm of Harappan stonecraft—from thick ramparts and stepped bastions to ceremonial gateway portals and paved pathways.

Controlled entryways and elevated plazas reveal how spatial hierarchy governed daily movement, defense, and public gatherings within the ancient city.

02 / Hydraulic Engineering

Massive Interconnected Step-Reservoirs

Surrounded by an arid landscape with scarce rainfall, Dholavira's survival depended upon what is recognized as one of the most sophisticated rainwater harvesting networks of the ancient world. Flanked by two seasonal monsoon streams—the Manhar and Mansar—the city engineered deep stone-cut reservoirs directly into its urban fabric.

Hydraulic infrastructure: Deep stone-cut reservoirs and inlet channels encircling the citadel ramparts.

The 3D model reconstructs the colossal scale of these stone reservoirs, complete with inlet bunds, settling channels, and stepped embankments. In the reconstruction, water surfaces reflect desert light against massive stone retaining walls—illustrating how hydraulic utility and defensive fortifications were harmoniously integrated into a single civic system.

03 / Human Scale & Atmosphere

Inhabiting the Ancient Landscape

Transitioning from analytical aerial surveys to ground level shifts the perspective from cartographic observation to personal immersion. At human eye-height, the monumental scale of the masonry becomes tangible—the towering ramparts, deep reservoir depressions, and long stone thoroughfares establish a palpable sense of physical presence.

First-person perspective: Reservoir embankments, stone bastions, and atmospheric desert haze at human eye-level.

Natural directional sunlight, atmospheric haze, and subtle desert dust ground the architectural geometry in its physical reality. The ground-level viewpoint demonstrates how 3D environment art transforms archaeological artifacts into living spatial narratives that can be directly experienced.
